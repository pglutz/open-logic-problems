## Statement

The Stable Forking Conjecture proposes that in a **stable** theory, all forking is witnessed
by a stable formula: if $\operatorname{tp}(a/Ab)$ forks over $A$, then there is a stable
formula $\varphi(x,y) \in \operatorname{tp}(a/Ab)$ such that $\varphi(x,b)$ already forks over
$A$.

## Definitions

- **Stable theory**: a complete theory $T$ such that no formula has the order property — no
  $\varphi(x,y)$ and tuples $(a_i)_{i<\omega}, (b_i)_{i<\omega}$ with
  $\models \varphi(a_i, b_j) \iff i < j$.
- **Forking**: a syntactic/semantic independence notion generalizing linear independence and
  algebraic independence; $p$ *forks over* $A$ if it implies a finite disjunction of formulas
  each of which divides over $A$.
- **Stable formula**: a formula $\varphi(x,y)$ that does not have the order property, even
  though the ambient theory $T$ might not itself be stable.

## Known Partial Results

- The conjecture is known to hold in several restricted settings, including theories of finite
  Morley rank and certain classes of superstable theories.
- It is known to be closely tied to structural questions about the geometry of forking
  (e.g. one-basedness and the presence of definable group/field structure), and much of the
  partial progress comes from analyzing these special geometric configurations rather than a
  fully general argument.
- No counterexample is known within stable theories; the conjecture remains open in general.

- Various survey articles and lecture notes in stability theory discuss the conjecture and its
