## Statement

Fraïssé's Conjecture (proved by Laver in 1971) states that the class of countable linear orders is well-quasi-ordered by embeddability. Shore proved that Fraïssé's Conjecture implies $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. This question asks whether this implication can be reversed. That is, does $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ suffice to prove Fraïssé's Conjecture? More generally, what is the precise strength (in the sense of reverse mathematics) of Fraïssé's Conjecture?

## Definitions

- A *well-quasi-order (WQO)* is a quasi-order with no infinite strictly descending sequence and no infinite antichain (set of pairwise incomparable elements).
- $\mathsf{ATR_0}$ is the subsystem of second-order arithmetic axiomatizing arithmetical transfinite recursion—roughly, the ability to iterate arithmetical comprehension along any well-order.

## Known Partial Results

- Laver's original proof of Fraïssé's Conjecture requires $\Pi^1_2$-comprehension.
- Montalbán proved that Fraïssé's Conjecture is provable from $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension.
- It is known that Fraïssé's Conjecture does not imply $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension (because Fraïssé's Conjecture is a $\Pi^1_2$-statement and $\Pi^1_1$-comprehension cannot be equivalent to any $\Pi^1_2$-statement).
- Shore proved that Fraïssé's Conjecture implies $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ so $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ is a lower bound on the reverse mathematical strength of Fraïssé's Conjecture.
