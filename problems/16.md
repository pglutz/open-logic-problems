## Statement

Blass proved in $\mathsf{ZF}$ that if every vector space has a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds. However, his proof depends on vector spaces over *all* fields having bases. Does the implication still hold if we only consider vector spaces over a single fixed field? 

There are really two versions of this question.
1. Is it provable in $\mathsf{ZF}$ that if there is *some* field for which every vector space has a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds?
2. Is there some specific natural field $F$ such as $\mathbb{F}_2, \mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{R},$ or $\mathbb{C}$ such that it is provable in $\mathsf{ZF}$ that if every vector space over $F$ has a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds? 

Note that a positive answer to the second question implies a positive answer to the first question.

## Known Partial Results

- Blass proved in $\mathsf{ZF}$ that if every vector space has a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds [^Bla83].
- Keremedis showed in $\mathsf{ZFA}$ that if, in every vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$, every generating set contains a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds [^Ker96].
- Keremedis showed in $\mathsf{ZFA}$ that if every vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$ has a basis then for each $n$, every well-ordered family of $n$-element sets has an infinite subset with a choice function [^Ker01].
- Morillon showed over $\mathsf{ZFA}$ that if every vector space over $\mathbb{F}_2$ has a basis then the Axiom of Choice for families of sets of size $2$ holds [^Mor09].

## Notes

There is a related question stemming from Blass's theorem. Blass showed in $\mathsf{ZF}$ that if every vector space has a basis then the Axiom of Choice holds. However, his proof does not work over $\mathsf{ZFA}$, i.e. $\mathsf{ZF}$ with the Axiom of Extensionality modified to allow atoms. It is an open question whether the implication holds over $\mathsf{ZFA}$.
