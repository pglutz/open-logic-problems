## Statement

The **Partition Principle** ($\mathsf{PP}$) states: if there is a surjection from a set $A$
onto a set $B$ (equivalently, $B$ admits a partition into at most $|A|$ nonempty blocks), then
there is an injection from $B$ into $A$. In $\mathsf{ZF}$ (without choice), $\mathsf{PP}$
follows from the Axiom of Choice ($\mathsf{AC}$), but whether

$$
\mathsf{ZF} + \mathsf{PP} \implies \mathsf{AC}
$$

is one of the oldest open problems concerning choice principles, going back to the early
1900s.

## Definitions

- **$\mathsf{ZF}$**: Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory without the Axiom of Choice.
- **Partition Principle ($\mathsf{PP}$)**: for sets $A, B$, if there is a surjection
  $A \twoheadrightarrow B$, then there is an injection $B \hookrightarrow A$.
- **Axiom of Choice ($\mathsf{AC}$)**: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

## Known Partial Results

- $\mathsf{PP}$ is known to imply several weaker choice-like consequences.
- $\mathsf{PP}$ has been shown equivalent to $\mathsf{AC}$ under various additional hypotheses,
  but no such additional hypothesis is known to be eliminable in general.
- The problem has resisted both a proof of the implication and the construction of a
  permutation/symmetric or forcing model separating $\mathsf{PP}$ from $\mathsf{AC}$.

## Notes

This problem is notable for its longevity — it has remained open for over a century despite
being one of the most natural questions one can ask about weak forms of choice, and despite
extensive work relating $\mathsf{PP}$ to essentially every other known choice principle.
