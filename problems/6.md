## Statement

Let $H_3$ be the (triangle-free) Henson graph: the countable, homogeneous, universal
$K_3$-free graph. Is $H_3$ **pseudofinite** — that is, does every sentence true in $H_3$ also
hold in some finite graph (equivalently, is $H_3$ elementarily equivalent to a structure
arising as an ultraproduct of finite graphs)?

## Definitions

- **Henson graph $H_3$**: the unique (up to isomorphism) countable graph that is homogeneous,
  triangle-free, and universal for countable triangle-free graphs — every countable
  triangle-free graph embeds into it.
- **Pseudofinite structure**: a structure that is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of
  finite structures (equivalently, every sentence true in it is true in some finite structure
  of the same signature).

## Known Partial Results

- The analogous question is resolved for the ordinary (triangle-permitting) random graph,
  which is pseudofinite.
- Various structural and combinatorial properties of $H_3$ relevant to pseudofiniteness (e.g.
  related to Ramsey-type and homogeneity properties of $K_3$-free graphs) have been studied,
  but a resolution of pseudofiniteness itself remains open.

## Additional References

- Henson, C. W. "A family of countable homogeneous graphs." *Pacific Journal of Mathematics*,
  1971 (introduces the Henson graphs).
- Various papers on pseudofinite structures and generalized Fraïssé constructions discuss this
  question; readers should consult recent model theory literature on pseudofiniteness for
  up-to-date status.

## Notes

This question sits at the intersection of model theory and finite combinatorics/finite model
theory, and is a natural test case for understanding which homogeneous structures built by
Fraïssé-style constructions are pseudofinite.
