## Statement

Let $\mathcal{H}_3$ denote the universal triangle free graph, also known as the *Henson graph*. Is $\mathcal{H}_3$ pseudofinite? More generally, is the universal $K_n$-free graph pseudofinite for each $n$?

## Definitions

- A graph $G$ *omits* a graph $H$ if $H$ is not isomorphic to any subgraph of $G$. Note that this is weaker than requiring that $H$ is not isomorphic to any minor of $G$.
- For any $n > 2$, the *Henson graph* $\mathcal{H}_n$ denotes the *universal $K_n$-free graph*, i.e. the unique-up-to-isomorphism countable graph which omits $K_n$ and into which every countable graph omitting $K_n$ embeds. 
- A structure $A$ is *pseudofinite* if every first order sentence that it satisfies is also satisfied by some finite structure in the same signature. Equivalently, $A$ is elementarily equivalent to an ultraproduct of finite structures.
