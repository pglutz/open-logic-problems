## What do the statuses mean?

- **Open** — unresolved, as far as we know.
- **Closed** — resolved, with a generally accepted proof.
- **No consensus** — a proof has been claimed but is not yet broadly verified or accepted.

## What do the exclamation marks (impact) mean?

They rate expected **impact** — roughly, how much the community would be expected to care if
the problem were resolved — rather than pure difficulty. Difficulty plays a role, but a
problem can be very hard without being especially consequential, or important without
necessarily being the hardest thing in its area.

- **!** — notable. Every problem listed on this site, including single-exclamation-mark ones,
  is a genuine, worthwhile open problem — this rating is not a judgment that a problem is
  trivial or unimportant.
- **!!** — substantial: resolution would likely be publishable in a strong journal
  (Advances-level or above).
- **!!!** — landmark: resolution would be of award-level significance.

This rubric is inherently subjective — treat it as a rough guide, not a strict rule.

## What problems can be included?

As a rule of thumb, a problem should have appeared in a published paper or book. We make
exceptions for problems appearing only in preprints or other unpublished sources, provided a
brief justification is given — this is asked for directly when you
[submit a new problem](/problems/new).

## How do I suggest an edit to a problem?

Every problem page has an editor where you can revise its details and prose, add a short
description of your change, and submit it for review. You do not need a GitHub account.
Changes are reviewed by the maintainer before they go live.

## How do I propose a new problem?

Use [Submit a New Problem](/problems/new). You'll fill in the same kind of structured details
(area, impact, reference, etc.) plus a note on where the problem was published. It goes
through the same review process as edits before appearing on the site.

## Can I comment on a problem?

Yes — each problem page has a comment section. A lightweight sign-up (no GitHub account
needed) is required to post.
